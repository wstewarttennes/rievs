Classic car EV conversion

How we go about converting classic and project cars to electric without wrecking what you liked about them.

Why convert a classic car to electric

A lot of classics end up better as EVs than they ever were as gas cars. They start every time. They stop leaking on the garage floor. No tune-ups, and the torque is there from nothing. Most of them are light and mechanically simple with no computers to integrate, which is why they're such satisfying things to convert.

The idea is to leave the parts you actually care about alone. Body, interior, how it sits. What changes is underneath.

What makes a good candidate

  • Light and simple — less weight means a smaller pack for the same range.
  • Solid structure — the car has to carry the battery weight safely, so any rust repair happens first.
  • Room for batteries — engine bay, trunk, and the space where the fuel tank lived.
  • A manual gearbox — the simplest path is keeping it and mating the motor to the bellhousing.

Keeping the character

Weight and balance

Where modules go changes how the car handles. We place the pack to keep weight low and close to the original balance, then check suspension and brakes against the new weight.

Gauges and dash

The original gauges obviously weren't built for a battery, but they can usually be kept and driven off the new system. Some people add a small second display for state of charge and pack data. On our Ranger we added a small drive selector knob and kept the original dash.

Charging without cutting the body

The charge port can often live behind the original fuel door, so the car looks untouched from the outside.

Heat, brakes, and steering

Anything that ran off the engine — vacuum brake assist, power steering, cabin heat — gets an electric replacement or is deliberately left out.

Reversibility

If originality matters to you, tell us early. We can plan the conversion around the car's original mounting points and keep the original engine parts so the change isn't permanent.

What the conversion includes

  • AC motor and inverter sized to the car
  • Battery pack design, enclosures, and mounting
  • Battery management, contactors, fusing, and high-voltage safety
  • Onboard charger and J1772 or NACS charge port
  • 12V system, gauges, and dash integration

Cost and range

Both depend on the same handful of things — pack size, what the car weighs, and how much fabrication it takes to fit everything properly. We quote per car, after talking through how you actually drive it. See the line-by-line cost breakdown.

FAQ

Common questions

Q1Does an EV conversion ruin a classic car's value?

It depends on the car. For rare, numbers-matching cars, a reversible conversion that keeps the original parts is the safer approach. For drivers and project cars, a well-done conversion can make the car far more usable. Talk to us before you start.

Q2Can I keep the original gauges?

Often, yes. Original gauges can be kept and paired with the new system or a discreet added display.

Q3How long does a classic car EV conversion take?

It varies with the car's condition and the amount of fabrication. We give a timeline with the spec and quote.
